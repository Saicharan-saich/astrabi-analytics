## What must appear on the invoice

Legally required for a UK sole trader:

1. The word "Invoice"
2. A unique, sequential invoice number
3. Your full legal name and your trading name
4. Your address and contact details
5. The customer's name and address
6. A clear description of what is being charged for
7. The supply date (when the service was provided) and the invoice date
8. The amount charged and the total due

Because you are not VAT registered, the invoice must **not** show a VAT amount
or a VAT number. Add the line "Not VAT registered" so it is unambiguous.

## Practical notes

- **Number sequentially and never reuse a number.** `QI-2026-001`, `-002`, and
  so on. Gaps and duplicates are what make an invoice set look constructed after
  the fact.
- **Keep your records for 5 years** after the 31 January submission deadline for
  the relevant tax year.
- **Register for Self Assessment** if you have not already — required once
  trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year.
- **Overseas customers:** invoice in GBP and let them handle conversion. Note
  that the customer may be required to withhold tax in their own country;
  agree who bears that cost before invoicing.
- **Send as PDF**, and keep the sent email — it evidences the date.
- **Keep the remittance advice or bank statement line** showing the payment
  arriving. The invoice proves you asked; the statement proves they paid, and
  the second one is what carries weight.

## Order of operations

1. Agree the price with the customer by email — that email is your paper trail.
2. Send the user agreement with Option A (paid) completed. Get it signed.
3. Send the invoice referencing the agreement.
4. Save: signed agreement, invoice PDF, and proof of payment received.

Those three documents together are one complete evidence package per customer.
